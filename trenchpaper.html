IN SHORT

A card game about the Robinhood Chain Pons Deck. 775 cards covering 155 real projects, the tools and the tactics. Build 40, play 10 turns, highest market cap wins.

It is playable now, in your browser — no wallet, no token, nothing to buy. Matches against a person run a day a turn, and can be played for a stake or for nothing.

$PDECK has launched. The contract address is on this site and in our Telegram. When it does, most of what the game earns is spent buying it and burning it, and every burn is a transaction anybody can check.

Holding never changes what you may put in a deck. It changes how much of a win you keep — from 25% burned down to 5%.

01

A MATCH

Two players, 10 turns each, highest market cap at the end. Every turn you are given a marketing budget, and every card costs some of it to play. The budget grows: it starts at $40K and reaches $400K on the last turn.

Unspent budget comes off your market cap. Not metaphorically — it is subtracted when the turn ends. Sitting on money is not caution here, it is a payment, and it is the rule that stops a match being a race to hoard.

Only a project opens a position, and only a position pumps. A position pays its pump into your market cap at the end of every turn, so a project played on turn two is worth far more than the same card on turn nine — it is not what it costs, it is how many payouts are left.

A full portfolio does not block a project — it asks you to close one. What that position already made stays yours; what you give up is everything it would have paid from here. That choice is the reason the cap exists.

03

A DECK

40 cards, one copy of each, out of the cards you own. There is no deck budget — a card is paid for when you play it — so the question is not what you can afford to own but whether you have something worth doing with every turn’s money.

You draw a median of 31 cards in a match, so at this size you see nearly all of it. What you build is what you get.

One thing the numbers are blunt about: decks drawn from packs run from 21% to 74% against each other, measured. So a rank here is deck plus player rather than player. That is right for pairing people and a poor basis for anything that reads as merit.

04

OWNING CARDS

Cards come out of packs and belong to a wallet, not a browser. There are two products: a single card at 0.03 SOL, which guarantees nothing; and a pack of 10 at 0.25 SOL with one slot guaranteed epic or better — ten cards for the price of just over eight singles, which is what a pack is for. A deck mint of sixty was a third, and went: sixty cards is twenty transactions and about two minutes, which is longer than a request lives.

Every draw is against the whole set, so a card you already hold can come out again. Odds that stop being true once your collection fills up are not odds.

Holding $PDECK never changes what you may put in a deck.

That was measured before it was decided. When decks had a points budget, one built on 110 points beat one built on 80 in 86% of matches. Selling deck power in a game people bet on is not selling a stronger deck — it is selling the result of the bet.

05

PLAYING PEOPLE

Matches are correspondence: a day a turn, so a match runs over a week and nobody has to be anywhere. When the clock runs out your turn ends — not the match. At one minute, forfeiting would mean a bad connection costs a stake, and losing a whole turn’s budget is punishment enough.

Every match therefore ends. A match both players abandon still resolves, as a draw, after twenty windows. There is no stuck-funds case in this system and so no refund path, no dispute window and no timeout to argue about — which is normally the hard half of putting money on a game.

The engine decides on the server. In the maker’s previous project the card check was a filter in the interface, and a direct call could play anything.

06

THE LADDER

Rank is a plain Elo starting at 1000, and only staked matches move it. Friendly matches leave it alone: dropping a rank has to cost a stake rather than an afternoon.

The attack on a rating with money behind it runs downward. Nobody inflates a rating, because a higher one buys harder opponents. What pays is losing on purpose until the rank is low and then taking an expensive seat against somebody who genuinely belongs there. Three things are arranged against that, and none works alone.

The lagging mean is the substance of it. Throw five matches and a quarter of the fall registers; decline honestly over thirty and all of it does. Falling is free when it is real and slow when it is chosen.

You may refuse a challenger far above you, and far below you — that second half is the one people forget. Taking a much weaker player’s stake is exactly what the band exists to stop. What you may not refuse is an equal, or the band becomes a way to sit in a seat and only take the matches you fancy.

07

STAKES AND ESCROW

Stakes are 0.1, 0.5, 1, 5, 10 SOL. Both stakes are in escrow before the match exists — not after the first move, because a player who has seen their opening hand and then decides whether to pay is not staking anything.

The escrow is custodial, and that is not dressed up.

One wallet holds both stakes. An on-chain program would hold them perfectly and still have to be told who won, because the match is decided off-chain — so the trust moves to a key rather than away from it. The only way round that is reimplementing every rule in a contract, which would be a second implementation of the rules and the thing the maker’s last project died of.

What is offered instead is that every payment and every payout is a transaction you can look at, linked from the match that produced it.

WHAT COULD GO WRONG

The escrow is a hot wallet. The server pays out without a human, so it holds a key that can move funds. Whatever is on it is the whole of what is at risk, which is why it holds what is in play and not more.

A rank is deck plus player. Somebody climbing may have minted better rather than played better. Fine for pairing people; worth remembering before prizes hang off it.

Sandbagging is expensive, not impossible. The lagging mean and the tier gate make a staged fall cost sixty SOL of real matches at weights that move a rank the whole way. That is a price, not a wall.

One task is taken on trust. X charges per follower read, so following cannot be checked automatically. It is marked as declared rather than verified, and it is removed by hand when it is not true.

The art references real projects and real people. The NFT metadata stays editable so a picture can come down if a rights holder objects. That is a repair and not a promise: marketplaces cache images hard, and some caches never refresh.
